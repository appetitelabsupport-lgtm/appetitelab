Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 18, 2026 · Applies to the AppetiteLab iOS app and web app

The short version

  • Your food log (including custom drinks, drink add-ons and food modifications), saved meals, weight log, profile and targets, reminders, caffeine log and settings are stored only on your device. We don't have accounts or servers that receive your diary.
  • When you scan or type a barcode, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts and FatSecret to look up the product. When you search for a food, the words you type are sent to USDA FoodData Central, Open Food Facts and FatSecret to find matches. Barcode images are read on your device and are not uploaded.
  • If you use AI food scan (a plate or menu photo) or bloodwork scan (a lab-report photo) and tap continue on the consent sheet, that photo is sent to AppetiteLab’s Cloudflare service and then to OpenAI, a third-party AI provider, for that request only. The photo is not stored by AppetiteLab. See “Photos sent to AI” below.
  • Subscriptions are processed by Apple and managed with RevenueCat. We never see your payment details. Price, length, and any free trial are shown before you confirm. The plan renews until you cancel in your Apple ID settings.
  • If you turn on Apple Health sync, the calories and water you log are written to Health on your device. If you choose Apple Health as your activity source, the app reads your active energy, resting energy and steps on your device only. If you turn on weight sync, your weigh-ins are written to and read from Health (Body Mass) on your device. Apple Health data stays on your device and is never sent to us or used for advertising. It is sent to OpenAI only if you turn on “Share Apple Health with coach” (off by default); see “Coach (optional)” below.
  • If you connect WHOOP, your daily WHOOP calories, strain and steps pass through our small sign-in service to your device. We don't store them.
  • If you turn on AI Coach and tap Allow, your coach messages and a summary of your app data (Apple Health data only if you separately opt in) are sent to OpenAI to write replies. See “Coach (optional)” below.
  • Protocol (compound schedules, dose logs, vial inventory, and certificate-of-analysis files) and saved bloodwork values stay on your device. The tab is for adults 18+ and is a tracker only — not medical advice, and not a way to buy or source compounds.
  • If you send a feature request or a compound-library request, the text (and, if you leave the box checked, your device’s user agent, app version, and an app-generated device id) is emailed to AppetiteLab through Web3Forms and our feedback service. We do not upload a photo with that form.
  • We don't sell your data, show ads, or track you across other apps or websites.

Data stored on your device

Foods you log (names, calories, servings, optional protein/carbs/fat and other nutrients, meal and date), water you log, foods you save to My Foods, your daily goals, your list of recent foods, and cached product information are saved in the app's local storage on your device. This data is not uploaded to us. It is removed if you delete the app, use "Erase all data" in Settings, or clear the app's data. You can export a copy at any time from Settings. Depending on your device settings, your device backups (for example iCloud Backup) may include this data; those backups are controlled by you and Apple.

The same applies to the profile you enter to calculate your targets (units, sex, age, height, current and goal weight, goal, activity level, diet style and any targets you set yourself), the reminders you add in the Calendar (their names, optional amounts and units, schedule, notes, and whether you marked them taken or skipped), the caffeine you log (amounts, drink names, your quick-add items and your limit), the weigh-ins you enter (date and weight), your saved meals, and the settings for quick log, portion sizes, smart reminders and the weekly recap (including the typical meal times the app works out from your own log to time its reminders). This information is used only on your device to show your targets, calendar and totals. It is not sent to us, to RevenueCat, to Apple Health or to anyone else, except the optional AI Coach summary described below. It is included in Export and removed by "Erase all data". AppetiteLab only shows the reminder details you type; it doesn't give medical or dosing advice.

Protocol data is stored the same way, only on this device: the compounds you add, dose amounts and times you enter, schedules, dose logs, vial inventory and notes, and any certificate-of-analysis photo or PDF you attach. Saved bloodwork panels (the marker names and values you keep after review) stay on this device. None of that Protocol or bloodwork diary is uploaded, and it is not included in AI Coach requests. It is removed by "Erase all data".

Supplement lookups: NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database

If you scan or type the barcode of a supplement, the app sends that barcode number to the Dietary Supplement Label Database run by the U.S. National Institutes of Health (Office of Dietary Supplements) to look up the label. Like any web request, this includes your device's IP address and basic technical information. Nothing else is sent. See the NIH privacy notice.

Barcode lookups: Open Food Facts

To identify a product, the app sends the barcode number you scanned or typed to the free, open database Open Food Facts (operated by the non-profit Open Food Facts association). Like any web request, this includes your device's IP address and basic technical information. Product photos are loaded from Open Food Facts' image servers. No food diary data is sent. See the Open Food Facts privacy policy.

Food search: USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts

When you type in the food search box, the search words are sent to USDA FoodData Central (api.nal.usda.gov, run by the U.S. Department of Agriculture through api.data.gov) and to Open Food Facts (world.openfoodfacts.org) to fetch matching foods. These requests contain only the search words, an app key that identifies the app (not you), and, like any web request, your device's IP address and basic technical information. No account, food diary or other personal data is sent. Recent results are kept briefly in memory on your device. See the USDA privacy policy and api.data.gov. Nutrition for restaurant menu items (for example McDonald's and Smoothie King) is built into the app from the chains' published nutrition information, so searching them sends nothing extra.

Food search and barcodes: FatSecret

Food searches and barcode numbers are also sent to FatSecret (fatsecret.com) through a small AppetiteLab service hosted on Cloudflare, which keeps the FatSecret key off your phone. The request contains only the search words or barcode number; FatSecret sees our service, not your device or IP address. Our service doesn't log or keep a record of who searched. No account, food diary or other personal data is sent. See the FatSecret privacy policy. Food data is powered by fatsecret.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, reminder notifications are scheduled on your device by iOS (local notifications) and show the reminder name, amount and notes you entered. Optional smart meal reminders, the weigh-in reminder and the weekly recap reminder (all off by default) are scheduled the same way, on your device. No push service or server is involved. In the web version, reminders are shown by your browser only while AppetiteLab is open. You can turn notifications off any time in iOS Settings → Notifications → AppetiteLab (or your browser's site settings).

Camera and photo library

The camera and photo library are used to read barcodes, take or pick progress photos, attach a certificate of analysis to a vial you track, and — only after you agree — photograph or pick a plate, menu, or lab report for AI scan. Barcode images are processed on your device and are not stored or uploaded. Progress photos and certificate-of-analysis files are saved only on your device; they are not sent to us or to any AI service, are included when you export, and are removed by “Erase all data”.

Photos sent to AI (optional)

Two features send a photo off your device, and only after a consent sheet that names the third party:

  • AI food scan (Pro). A plate or menu photo, plus the scan mode and a short note of remaining calories and macros for that day, is sent to AppetiteLab’s service at appetitelab-coach.appetitelab.workers.dev/food-scan and from there to OpenAI to estimate foods and macros. You review and edit every item before anything is logged. The numbers are estimates.
  • Bloodwork scan (Pro, Protocol tab, 18+). A lab-report photo and an optional panel date are sent to appetitelab-coach.appetitelab.workers.dev/bloodwork and from there to OpenAI to read marker names and values. You review and edit every value before it is saved on this device. This does not diagnose, treat, or interpret the panel for you.

The request also includes an app-generated user id (your account code, or a random id if you have none) and your subscription tier, used only to enforce a daily scan limit. AppetiteLab’s service stores that daily count. It does not store the photo, the extracted text, or a copy of the result. OpenAI’s API is not used to train their models. See the OpenAI privacy policy. You can decline the consent sheet and type values or foods yourself. Erase all data clears the saved consent so the sheet is shown again.

Quick log and dictation

Quick log turns what you type (for example "two eggs, toast and a coffee") into foods on your device; the food names are then looked up like a normal food search (see above). In the iOS app, dictation uses the iPhone keyboard's microphone button, which is Apple's keyboard dictation and is covered by Apple's privacy policy; AppetiteLab does not record audio. In the web version, the microphone button uses your browser's speech recognition, which some browsers (for example Chrome) process on the browser maker's servers; it is only used while the microphone button is on, and you can always type instead.

Subscriptions: Apple and RevenueCat

AppetiteLab Pro and Pro+ are auto-renewing subscriptions purchased through your Apple ID. The price, billing period, and any free trial (Pro includes a 3-day trial; Pro+ does not) are shown on the purchase screen before you confirm. Payment is charged to your Apple ID. The subscription renews unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the end of the current period in Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions. Deleting the app or using Erase all data does not cancel the subscription. Apple processes the payment; we never receive your card or billing details. We use RevenueCat to check whether your subscription is active. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app user ID generated on your device (shown in the app as your "account code", for example AL-7K4Q-M2XD, so you can share it if someone gives you free access; it contains nothing about you), your purchase and subscription history (such as product, dates, price and trial status) and basic device information (such as app version, OS version, country/currency). This is used only to provide and restore your subscription. See the RevenueCat privacy policy and Apple's privacy policy.

Apple Health (optional)

If you turn on "Sync calories & water to Health" (Settings → Apple Health) and grant permission, the app writes the calories (dietary energy) of each food you log, and the water (dietary water) you log, to the Health app on your device.

If you turn on "Sync with Apple Health" on the Weight screen and grant permission, the app writes the weigh-ins you enter to Health as Body Mass and reads Body Mass from Health to fill in days you didn't enter in AppetiteLab.

If you choose Apple Health as your activity source (Settings → Activity) and grant permission, the app reads your daily active energy, resting energy and step count to show calories burned and, if you turn it on, to adjust your calorie goal. This happens on your device; Health data is not sent to us, is sent to the AI Coach (OpenAI) only if you turn on Share Apple Health with coach, is never used for advertising, and you can revoke access at any time in the Health app or iOS Settings.

WHOOP (optional)

If you connect WHOOP (Settings → Activity → Connect WHOOP), you sign in on WHOOP's website and allow AppetiteLab to read your WHOOP cycles (daily calories burned, strain, heart-rate summary and steps). WHOOP requires a secret key for this that can't safely be put inside an app, so sign-in and data requests go through a small AppetiteLab service hosted on Cloudflare. That service only passes requests between your device and WHOOP: it does not store your WHOOP data, your WHOOP sign-in or any identifier, and it doesn't log request contents (Cloudflare may keep standard technical logs such as IP address). Your WHOOP sign-in tokens are stored on your device in the iOS Keychain, and the daily numbers are cached on your device so they can be shown offline. Tapping Disconnect deletes the tokens from your device and revokes AppetiteLab's access at WHOOP. See the WHOOP privacy policy.

Workouts, Live Activity and widgets

While a workout you started is running, AppetiteLab can show the elapsed time, current set and rest countdown on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island (a Live Activity), and Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets can show your calories, macros and water. These are drawn on your device from data stored on your device; nothing is sent to us. The Live Activity ends when you finish or cancel the workout.

If you use Apple Health (for example as your activity source) and grant permission, the workout screen reads your current heart rate and active energy from Health during a workout you start, and when you log a past workout it reads your average heart rate and active energy from that workout’s time. With Apple Health as your activity source, the app also reads when your workouts happened to place them on your activity days. This Health data stays on your device, is not sent to us, is never used for advertising, and is sent to the AI Coach only as described in the Coach section below.

Cardio (optional)

If you start a run, walk, or ride, AppetiteLab uses your location to measure distance, pace or speed, elevation, and the route on a map. While that session is running, location can keep updating if you leave the app or lock the screen, and it stops when the session ends. You can also connect a Bluetooth heart-rate strap, or read a recent heart rate from Apple Health. The recorded route, GPS points, and heart-rate samples stay on your device. They are not sent to us. Map tiles are loaded from OpenFreeMap (map data © OpenStreetMap contributors) only to draw the map; like any web request, this includes your device’s IP address and the map area being shown, but not your route or location history. If you turn on Snap to roads while planning a route, each new segment sends only its two endpoints to our routing service so the line can follow paths. Those two points are not stored. The snapped line is saved on your device. Turning Snap off, or planning while offline, keeps the segment as a straight line on the device.

Coach (optional)

Coach tips, on-device chat and Basic coach run on your phone. Nothing in those modes is sent to us.

If you set Coach AI to AI Coach and tap Allow, the message you send and a summary of your app data are sent to OpenAI to write the reply. That summary can include your profile (name, age, sex, height, goal, phase, activity, diet style), calorie and macro targets, foods logged today and daily totals for recent days, your weight trend, weigh-ins you entered yourself, your workout plan, recent sessions and personal records, saved meals, recipes and recent foods, recent coach chat, and facts you asked the coach to remember (including allergies and injuries). Apple Health data is not included unless you turn on “Share Apple Health with coach” in Settings → Coach (off by default) and accept its separate prompt. Then the summary also includes daily sleep, steps, active energy and resting heart rate from the last 14 days, used only to write coach replies. Turn it off anytime to stop sending it. These are sent through a small AppetiteLab service hosted on Cloudflare, which keeps only an anonymous daily message count to enforce usage limits. AppetiteLab does not store that conversation. OpenAI’s API is not used to train their models. You can switch Coach AI to On-device or Off in Settings at any time. Off sends nothing. See the OpenAI privacy policy.

Protocol (optional, 18+)

The first time you open the Protocol tab, the app asks you to confirm that you are 18 or older and that you understand this is not medical advice. That acknowledgement is saved on this device until you use Erase all data. The tab lets you track compounds you enter yourself: schedules, dose logs, vial inventory, and optional certificate-of-analysis (COA) photos or PDFs you attach to a vial. Those logs, notes, and COA files stay on your device (or in your export if you choose to export). They are not uploaded to AppetiteLab and are not sent to OpenAI. Research summaries in the app are short, educational overviews with citations. They are not medical advice, they are not dosing instructions, and they do not recommend buying, selling, sourcing, or using any compound. Some compounds in the library are controlled substances where you live. AppetiteLab does not sell, ship, or endorse any product, and it does not help you obtain one. Compounds with no published human half-life are labeled illustrative.

Saved bloodwork values stay on your device, as described under “Photos sent to AI”. The scan does not diagnose or treat.

Feature and compound requests

Settings → Request a feature, and the compound request in Protocol, send the text you type to AppetiteLab’s support inbox. The message goes to Web3Forms (api.web3forms.com) and, as a second copy, to our Cloudflare feedback service, which emails it to appetitelabsupport@gmail.com. The message includes the request text (a feature idea, or a compound name and optional notes), the app version, build number, platform, and time, and an app-generated device id (your account code if the app has one, otherwise a random id stored on the device). If “Include app version & device” is checked — it is on unless you turn it off — the feature request also includes your device’s user-agent string. The form does not upload a photo and does not ask for your email address. We keep the email so we can read the request. Erase all data does not delete a message already sent. These requests are not medical advice and are not a way to buy or source a compound. See the Web3Forms privacy policy.

Children

AppetiteLab is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age required in your country or region), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The Protocol tab requires a one-time 18+ acknowledgement and is not intended for minors.

Your choices

  • Export a copy, or erase everything stored in the app, from Settings → Erase all data. That removes your food log, workouts, Protocol compound and dose logs, vial notes and COA files, bloodwork panels, the 18+ acknowledgement, and AI-scan consent on this device. AppetiteLab does not keep a server copy of that diary, so there is no separate account for us to delete. Canceling a subscription is separate and is done in your Apple ID settings.
  • Manage or cancel your subscription in your Apple ID settings (Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions).
  • Turn Apple Health sync off in the app, or revoke access in the Health app.
  • Disconnect WHOOP in Settings → Activity (this also revokes access at WHOOP), or remove AppetiteLab from your WHOOP account.
  • Switch Coach AI to On-device or Off in Settings to stop sending anything to OpenAI.
  • Turn off “Share Apple Health with coach” in Settings → Coach to stop sending Apple Health data to OpenAI.
  • Clear the Protocol 18+ acknowledgement by erasing app data (Settings → Erase all data); COA attachments are removed with your device data.
  • Deny or revoke camera access in iOS Settings; you can still type barcodes manually.
  • Edit or delete your profile, targets, reminders, caffeine log, weigh-ins and saved meals in the app at any time (Settings → Profile & Goals, the Calendar tab, the Caffeine card, the Weight card, Repeat & saved).
  • Turn reminder notifications off in iOS Settings → Notifications → AppetiteLab.

Recipes, plans, and workouts

Recipes, meal plans, grocery lists and workout logs are stored only on your device, the same way as your food log. They are included when you export and removed by “Erase all data”.

Coach tips are generated on your device from your own log. On-device coach chat, when your iPhone can run it, uses Apple’s on-device model; that prompt stays on the phone. Only AI Coach (above, after you tap Allow) sends anything to OpenAI.

Coaching and Protocol research text are not medical advice. Protocol tracking does not claim to treat, cure, or prevent disease.
